<Toggle summary={"What is a platform? Musings"}>

I imagine platforms as enabling people to provide services to each other rather than directly providing a service. Youtube does not create videos, Fiver does not do tasks, Amazon (at first) did not manufacture tables; it lowered the *barrier of entry* for Person A to provide a service for Person B. I guess this inherently creates a monetization or profit issue for the platform:

> Since no platform actually offers a service, what exactly does it charge people for?

Common answer of Silicon Valley: the content creators or service providers become product for some third party, i.e. advertisements. OR pay a subscription monthly fee for the privilege of reduced friction. Ok so what are relevant concerns to keep top of mind when considering platforms:

- **Public vs Private:** It almost does become inherent that platforms serve as a form of digital infrastructure. In enabling services, they might be encroaching onto duties of the government
- **Centralization and Accountability:** who is responsible for activity, the platform or the user? platforms won many legal battles to assert the latter. But as the *enabler of exchange* what is their role? Content moderation, etc
- **Network Effects and Lock In:** since strength of platform is in how many people on it, very prone to monopoly.

</Toggle>

# Themes and Takeaways

## Motifs in the Book:

Mostly, this book is resistance to “platform capitalism” embodied in people like Marc Andreesen. Pepi uses the term to describe the confluence of **techno-utopianism** (technology can solve all problems), **techno-determinism** (if it can be built, it will. If it will, it should / we should adjust to it), and **free-market capitalism.** Abbreviations: DU for digital utopianism, SV for silicon valley, PC for platform capitalism.

<Toggle summary={"Trying to decode this"}>

1. As noted above, platforms enable transactions. In this sense, **they create markets** that did not exist before at scale. They do this by making transactions easier.

</Toggle>

Motifs on how Pepi categorizes platform capitalism:

1. VCs spread misleading categorizations of tech to make money. DU is a series of myths that require debunking.
2. SV sells techy solutions to problems that are better solved through other means. DU systematically attacks and weakens alternatives that are more effective / socially healthy.
3. TU often paired with decentralization and libertarianism in ways that **sidestep institutions and political process**, thereby weakening it. Platforms weaken institutions and replace with an ideology of individualism (exchanges and interactions between two people on a network, no central body)
   1. Platforms and tech *are* centralized.

### Notes

Burst because:

3. Social backlash against privacy violations (IOS 13)

- Many Utopias skip ahead to a future with magical technology, skipping the *process* for how the messy present reaches the glorious future. Never tangle with real world social complexity.
  - Utopias about scrapping the old and starting afresh. “escapist fantasy” of making reality dissapear
- Pepi says Utopian zeal, which always promises incoming amazingness, sidesteps incremental imperfect change. If you question the “linear, positivist, and certain” (35) narrative of progress, retory is what the alternative is. Makes hard to resist.
- TU specifically present products as presently realized manifestations of utopian fix, profiting and marketing it

**Cyberculture** in Turner’s “From Counterculture to Cyberculture” book. Hippie Libs —Libertarianism—> PC

- Steven Levy and idea of cyberspace. Freedom from oppressive institutions; free exchange of information; decentralization. Fancifully described by John Perry Barlow.
- Built on idea of cyberspace as universal uncontrolled choice with no interference. Cyberspace exists vast and infinite, and no actor can control it. World that is “everywhere and nowhere”. Also makes appeal to nature and inevitability, that cyberspace is ordained
  - Pepi: rather than transcending politics, it weakens it with “positivist technocracy”.
  - Says freedom takes work to maintain, does not exist in and of itself without institutions

- In democracy, you have imperfect but well defined levers of recourse, while in PC decision making occurs in corporate boardroom with eye for profits. Different levers of feedback and change
- Monetization - platforms raise central issue of how these things make money. Eventual answer is you; democracy does not directly have this tension.
  - “digital serf tied to a rental cloud fiefdom you pay for with your personal data, your time, your attention”
  1. Expansion of broadband internet, faster cheaper and widely available
  2. Cloud computing made easy for producers to create and offer software to many users
  3. Mobile devices —> device wherever we go —> infinitely generated personal data for use
  4. Monetization of personal data. “do capitalism faster and better” replaced “make stuff free and open”. Co-opting by Venture Capital and economic elite hoping to make money. **Worst of both worlds.** Corporate America and wall street mixed with no-holds-barred libertarianism (de-cent)
